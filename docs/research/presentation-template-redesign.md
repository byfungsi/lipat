# Presentation template redesign

Research date: 26 October 2026. This report responds to visual review of the first Lipat collection, especially the Property, Hospitality, Culinary, Retail and Supply Chain contact sheet. It supersedes the earlier brief's preference for schematic artwork in visually led sectors. Source principles are distinguished below from original recommendations; none of the referenced artwork is licensed for redistribution merely because it is linked.

## Critique of the current work

The collection is functionally complete, but the visual design is too generic. Its weaknesses are visible before reading the words:

1. **The subject is absent.** A building icon cannot communicate light, space or material. A wave symbol cannot sell a guest experience. Three copies of a plate icon cannot make a culinary offering feel desirable. The imagery names the domain but does not tell its story.
2. **Everything receives similar emphasis.** Small headings, small body blocks, quiet cards and small symbols occupy comparable visual weights. There is little tension between a dominant focal point and supporting information.
3. **The decks feel like forms.** Repeated three-column cards, dot-and-line timelines and identical closing columns create a predictable administrative rhythm. Changing accent colors does not create a new design voice.
4. **White space is passive.** Large blank areas surround small content without a strong image or typographic gesture to anchor them. Empty space should intensify a focal point, not expose a lack of content.
5. **The same motif is repeated instead of developed.** Reusing a sun/wave or shopping bag on three cards offers no new information. Repetition is useful for grids and typography; a story needs changing evidence.
6. **The typography is overly cautious.** Nearly every slide uses a conventional title-plus-paragraph hierarchy. There is no editorial cover scale, dramatic short statement, captioned photograph, or truly dominant evidence number.
7. **Technical checks became an aesthetic proxy.** No overflow, valid PPTX and 30 unique IDs prove reliability. They do not prove composition, taste or subject relevance.

## References and concrete lessons

- [Pentagram: 520 Fifth Avenue](https://www.pentagram.com/work/520-fifth-avenue). The studio describes a neutral palette and restrained type that foreground architectural imagery, plus commissioned neighborhood photography with an editorial character. **Lipat interpretation:** property and architecture should show a large spatial image, then a material/detail image; do not surround each photograph with competing decorative panels.
- [Pentagram: Hiut](https://www.pentagram.com/work/hiut). Art direction connects the factory, makers, landscape, textures and unexpected details. The type and colors respond to the same industrial/craft story. **Lipat interpretation:** choose a coherent image world for each deck; show place, process and outcome instead of unrelated stock pictures.
- [Duarte: influencing through visual storytelling](https://www.duarte.com/blog/qa-how-to-influence-through-visual-storytelling-2/). Duarte recommends defining the script and visual language, including photography style, before building a visual presentation and its fuller document version. **Lipat interpretation:** shorten the visible slide copy and preserve operational detail in editable speaker notes. Establish the photographic direction before filling layout slots.
- [Pitch: presentation template gallery](https://pitch.com/templates). The official gallery distinguishes architecture portfolios, design portfolios, product launches, research reports and editorial decks as separate offerings. **Lipat interpretation:** do not treat thirty domains as palette variants; evaluate each deck against its own communication job. Individual template pages were inaccessible to the research browser, so this report does not claim detailed inspection of their internal layouts.
- [Adobe: graphic design process](https://www.adobe.com/learn/express/web/graphic-design-process). Adobe connects audience and aesthetic with distinct type roles, a simple light/dark/accent palette, and hierarchy through size, boldness and color. **Lipat interpretation:** make the cover title and primary image unmistakably dominant; choose one display voice per deck and keep secondary typography quiet.
- [Canva: resizing and cropping](https://www.canva.com/help/resize-and-crop/). Canva distinguishes scaling an entire image from cropping to focus on a specific subject or remove distractions. **Lipat interpretation:** art-direct the crop for the slide's frame. Do not stretch images or assume centered cropping preserves a face, object or building. Pick images with usable quiet areas when placing text beside them.
- [Storytelling with Data: two tips for better graphs](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/two-tips-for-better-graphs). The author distinguishes removing clutter from actively directing attention, describing evidence for improved clarity and recall when focus is added. **Lipat interpretation:** one key series or comparison gets the accent; contextual series are subdued. A useful takeaway headline should work with the chart.
- [Storytelling with Data: what clutter can we eliminate?](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/what-clutter-can-we-eliminate). The makeover removes heavy borders, redundant tables and unnecessary markers, and brings labeling closer to the data. **Lipat interpretation:** avoid making every chart a boxed dashboard tile; reserve the slide for one chart and one short implication.

## Seven original composition rules for the revision

These are proposed Lipat design decisions rather than measurements quoted from the sources.

1. **Compose a real image story.** Give visually led decks a coherent set of two or three images: an establishing scene, a close detail, and a process or human moment. Use original generated imagery or images with appropriate redistribution rights. Label fictional project imagery as illustrative in notes/metadata. Never imply it documents a real property, study, customer or beneficiary.
2. **Give images enough area to matter.** On image slides, use roughly half to three quarters of the canvas. A good crop is more valuable than a mosaic of tiny thumbnails. Keep important text editable and outside image pixels. Use a solid type area or image with genuinely quiet negative space; do not rely on a weak translucent overlay over busy detail.
3. **Use three different image compositions.** Full-height asymmetrical image with a narrow title column; panoramic image above a compact caption band; oversized editorial headline beside a tightly cropped material/subject detail. A fourth option is a clean contact sheet for a genuine assortment or process sequence. Avoid making all covers the same left-text/right-photo split.
4. **Build rhythm across six slides.** A useful sequence is image-led invitation → terse thesis or problem → clear evidence → image-led demonstration → decision/process → memorable closing statement. At least one slide should be very quiet and one should have dramatic scale. Do not force a photograph onto a data slide merely to satisfy an image count.
5. **Use bolder typography and less copy.** At 1280 × 720, begin cover titles around 76–100 logical units, short statement type around 60–80, body around 25–30 and captions around 17–20. These are starting points subject to actual measured layout, not license to shrink text to fit. Use intentional line breaks and short headings. Preserve two distinct type roles and a consistent alignment grid.
6. **Replace default cards with editorial structure.** Prefer open aligned columns, large section numerals, fine horizontal rules, a single dominant number, and contextual captions. Use cards only when a real object or comparison benefits from containment. Use one accent color actively; allow photographic tones to supply secondary color.
7. **Review at two scales and revise the weak slides.** At thumbnail scale, the focal point and deck identity must be obvious. At normal size, the crop, alignment, contrast and text must hold up. Review six slides together: if three consecutive slides have the same silhouette, change the composition. Inspect every cover, every image slide, and every chart; a contact sheet alone is insufficient.

## Practical acceptance gates

- The first five redesigned decks should demonstrate different voices: Architecture, Culinary, Venture, Revenue and Creative Portfolio. They exercise spatial photography, appetizing detail, product storytelling, data restraint and editorial image sequencing.
- Every template remains editable: photographs are separate image assets; no slide-sized raster containing typography or charts.
- Images are embedded and offline-capable, with source/provenance recorded. Runtime fetching must not be required to produce a complete deck.
- Each image receives a useful alt description and intentional fit/crop. Inspect the actual exported preview, not only the source photograph.
- There is no broad aesthetic claim based solely on automated tests. Publish a before/after visual comparison and inspect all 180 rendered slides, correcting the weakest examples.
- Avoid turning this revision into thirty photographic covers over the unchanged generic interiors. At least two interior compositions per deck should materially improve hierarchy, image storytelling or evidence design.
